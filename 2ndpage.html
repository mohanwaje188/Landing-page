The share market (also known as the stock market) is a marketplace where stocks (also called shares or equities) of publicly traded companies are bought and sold. It plays a critical role in the global economy by providing businesses with access to capital and allowing investors to buy ownership stakes in companies. Here's a detailed explanation of how the share market works: 1. What Are Shares? Shares represent a unit of ownership in a company. When you buy shares of a company, you essentially become a partial owner of that company, with the amount of ownership depending on how many shares you own relative to the total number of shares in circulation. Companies issue shares to raise money for expansion, operations, or other purposes. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 2. Stock Exchanges The share market operates through stock exchanges, which are centralized platforms where buying and selling of stocks takes place. Some of the most famous stock exchanges are: New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) NASDAQ London Stock Exchange (LSE) Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE) Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE) Stock exchanges provide a regulated environment where stocks can be traded efficiently, and they ensure fairness in the trading process. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 3. How the Share Market Works Initial Public Offering (IPO): When a private company decides to go public and raise capital from the public, it conducts an IPO. During this process, the company issues shares for the first time, and these shares become available for purchase on the stock market. Buying and Selling Shares: Once the shares are publicly traded, investors can buy and sell them through brokers. When you buy a share, you own a small part of the company, and if the company grows or performs well, the value of your shares increases. Conversely, if the company’s performance declines, the share price may decrease, leading to potential losses for investors. Stock Prices: The price of a stock fluctuates based on supply and demand. If many people want to buy a particular stock, the price will rise, and if more people are looking to sell than buy, the price will fall. Other factors affecting stock prices include company performance, industry trends, economic conditions, and investor sentiment. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 4. Participants in the Share Market Investors: These are individuals or institutions that buy shares in companies with the expectation that the value of those shares will increase over time, allowing them to sell at a profit. Investors can be divided into two main categories: Retail investors: Individuals who invest their personal money in stocks. Institutional investors: Large organizations such as mutual funds, pension funds, or hedge funds that manage large amounts of money and often invest in large quantities of stocks. Brokers: A stockbroker is an intermediary who buys and sells stocks on behalf of investors. Brokers may charge commissions or fees for facilitating trades. Market Makers: These are firms that ensure there is always liquidity in the stock market by being willing to buy or sell stocks at quoted prices. They help ensure that there is a continuous flow of transactions and that stocks are bought or sold when needed. ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 5. Types of Markets in the Share Market Primary Market: This is where securities are created, and companies issue new shares for the first time through IPOs. The money raised from the sale of these shares goes to the company for its business needs. Secondary Market: After shares are issued in the primary market, they are traded on the secondary market, which is the actual stock exchange. Investors trade shares among themselves, and the company does not receive any money from these transactions. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 6. Factors Influencing the Share Market Company Performance: The financial health of a company, including its revenue, profit, management quality, and future growth prospects, has a significant impact on the stock price. Economic Indicators: Economic conditions, such as interest rates, inflation, and GDP growth, influence market sentiment. A strong economy tends to lead to higher stock prices, while a weak economy can cause stock prices to fall. Political Factors: Government policies, elections, and political stability can affect investor confidence and the stock market. For example, changes in tax laws, trade policies, or regulations can impact corporate earnings and stock prices. Global Events: International events, such as natural disasters, pandemics (like COVID-19), or geopolitical tensions, can create uncertainty and cause market volatility. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 7. Risk and Reward The share market offers significant opportunities for profit but also carries inherent risks. The value of shares can fluctuate widely in response to a range of factors, and investors may lose money if they sell shares at a lower price than they paid for them. On the other hand, investing in the share market can offer higher returns compared to traditional savings accounts or bonds. Long-Term Growth: Historically, stock markets have provided long-term growth, outperforming other investment options like bonds or real estate. However, short-term volatility is common, and investors must be prepared for ups and downs. Diversification: One strategy to manage risk in the stock market is diversification. This involves spreading investments across different sectors or asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate) to reduce the risk of loss from any single investment. --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 8. Types of Shares Common Shares: Common stockholders have voting rights at annual meetings and may receive dividends, though the company is not required to pay dividends. They are last in line to receive assets if the company is liquidated. Preferred Shares: Preferred stockholders have a higher claim on assets and earnings than common shareholders. They receive dividends before common shareholders and have a fixed dividend rate, but typically do not have voting rights. ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- 9. Regulation and Safety The share market is heavily regulated to ensure transparency, fairness, and the protection of investors. In many countries, organizations like the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) in the U.S. oversee the markets and enforce rules regarding disclosure, trading practices, and fraud prevention.
